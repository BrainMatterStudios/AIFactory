# AIFactory 0.4.0 quality evidence and independent verification brief

**Date:** 2026-09-26

**Status:** Roadmap brief; later detailed design approval required

**Depends on:** Stable 0.3.0 Contract, Design IR, capability, gate, approval,
operational-evidence, and status schemas, plus recorded field evidence from at
least one supported T2 execution path

## Objective

Make behavioral verification independently observable, non-vacuous, and
replayable without giving a testing agent, test runner, browser provider, or
deployment system approval authority.

## Evidence basis

Operational validation proved that author-reported tests cannot serve as
authority and that verification must run outside the authoring agent. Separate
factory audits found recurring false-green patterns: empty or skipped suites,
unmatched filters, inert commands, absent populations, and authored proof that
could not demonstrate failure. The release generalizes the Stage 1 independent
verifier boundary into a provider-neutral product contract.

## User outcomes

- A reviewer can tell whether a required check executed, what it observed, and
  whether it was capable of detecting failure.
- Contract and Design obligations select proportionate quality profiles instead
  of launching every testing capability for every change.
- User-facing T2 work can receive fresh-context exploratory testing without
  allowing the tester to edit or approve the implementation.
- Browser, API, accessibility, visual, mobile, backend, and live-observation
  providers can emit one bounded evidence shape without becoming hard core
  dependencies.
- Status distinguishes a pull request, a merge, a deployment, and verified-live
  behavior when those observations exist.

## Planned scope

### Quality obligations

The core defines a versioned `quality-obligation-v1` artifact bound to the exact
Contract, Design IR, approved plan when present, base revision, and quality
profile. An obligation records:

- the behavior, invariant, or user journey being checked;
- the required positive, negative, regression, or exploratory controls;
- the required provider roles and capabilities;
- the target class and allowed environment without embedding credentials;
- the conditions that make the observation vacuous, untested, or unavailable;
- whether before-failure proof is meaningful and required; and
- explicit accepted gaps and non-goals.

The deterministic controller selects obligations from approved artifacts, risk
tier, and the actual changed surface. Profiles are additive: a project can make
requirements stricter, but a runner or pack cannot weaken the approved minimum.

### Quality-run evidence

Providers emit a bounded `quality-run-v1` observation. The controller binds the
record to the exact obligation and workspace and writes the authoritative
envelope. It includes:

- provider, runner, tool, and environment identities and versions;
- start, finish, timeout, retry, and flake information;
- selected, collected, executed, passed, failed, skipped, and unexecuted counts
  when the provider can observe them;
- positive and negative control results, including `fails_before` proof when
  the obligation requires it;
- bounded references to screenshots, traces, videos, HAR, console,
  accessibility, API, database-read, and other artifacts;
- structured findings, reproduction steps, coverage gaps, and explicit
  not-tested declarations; and
- redacted cost, latency, and artifact-size measures.

`evidence_state` is one of `executed`, `vacuous`, `untested`, or `unavailable`.
An executed observation separately records its pass or fail outcome. The
deterministic quality gate maps executed failure and required vacuity to block,
required unavailable evidence to unavailable, and optional gaps to an explicit
degraded projection. No absence becomes a pass.

### Independent testing sensor

The new `exploratory-tester` responsibility is distinct from test authoring and
implementation. It receives only the approved artifacts, changed surface,
allowed target, scoped test identity, declared tools, and prior authoritative
quality records. It does not receive the implementer's transcript.

The tester may explore the application and emit findings, reproduction steps,
artifact references, and coverage gaps. It cannot modify product code, rewrite
tests used as authority, create approvals, choose its own target or credentials,
or issue the quality-gate decision. Its reports remain findings-only sensor
input routed by deterministic controller policy.

### Provider boundary

AIFactory core owns schemas, binding, capability requirements, lifecycle,
routing, replay, redaction, and status. Provider packages own concrete browser,
API, accessibility, visual, mobile, backend, or live-observation execution.
Core retains zero hard third-party dependencies.

The first release must prove the contract with at least one non-toy browser path
and one API or backend path. A companion testing package or repository is
considered only after at least two materially different providers demonstrate a
stable interface and an independent release cadence has clear value.

### Post-delivery observations

Read-only providers may report `merged`, `deployed`, and `verified_live` against
exact source and runtime identities. These states never authorize merge or
deployment. Missing live evidence does not rewrite a pre-merge result; status
must say that live behavior is unverified whenever the Contract or operator
expects it.

## Authority and safety boundaries

- Agent-authored tests and agent-reported results are context, not authority.
- Required deterministic checks run under an independent verifier identity.
- Testing targets, accounts, network destinations, writable paths, and artifact
  limits are declared before execution and re-observed afterward.
- Test credentials remain external, scoped, short-lived where supported, and
  absent from retained reports and public artifacts.
- A testing provider cannot expand its quality profile, change an obligation,
  edit the candidate, approve it, publish it, deploy it, or promote itself.
- Raw pages, unrestricted recordings, production data, and unbounded logs are
  not retained by default.
- Live testing is read-only unless a separately approved Contract explicitly
  defines bounded reversible test data and cleanup.

## Compatibility and rollout

Existing `verify_cmd` projects remain valid. They receive a legacy deterministic
quality projection rather than being silently upgraded to browser or agentic
testing. Adoption of richer providers is explicit and capability-checked.

The release should stage scripted deterministic execution before exploratory
testing. An exploratory provider remains optional until field evidence shows
acceptable false-positive, false-negative, latency, cost, privacy, and operator
burden characteristics.

## Non-goals

- A universal proof that test-driven development chronology occurred;
- treating red-green history as proof of correctness;
- an autonomous QA approval authority;
- a hosted browser or device fleet;
- mandatory Playwright, browser-cloud, mobile, visual, or database dependencies;
- generating or repairing arbitrary product tests from the testing sensor;
- merge, deployment, production mutation, or synthetic-user management;
- replacing the final independent review panel; or
- a standalone testing product before the provider contract is proven.

## Entry criteria

- The 0.3.0 operational-validation gate is satisfied with retained evidence.
- Operational evidence and capability vocabularies are stable enough to extend
  without rewriting released artifacts.
- At least one representative UI flow and one API or backend flow are available
  for private, rollbackable field trials.
- Data-minimization rules define which browser, API, log, and runtime artifacts
  may be retained, exported, or projected into public-safe records.
- The detailed design defines bounded target, identity, network, credential,
  artifact, timeout, retry, and cleanup semantics.

## Adversarial evidence

Tests must cover zero collected tests, all-skipped suites, unmatched filters,
todo-only suites, missing fixtures, missing populations, commands that mask
failure through pipelines, source-grep masquerading as behavioral proof,
stale or cross-project reports, forged counts, swapped artifacts, unavailable
targets, false browser success, flaky retries, partial recordings, malicious
page content, prompt injection, credential leakage, oversized artifacts, target
escape, candidate mutation, and attempts by a testing provider to manufacture
approval.

The release corpus must contain at least one intentionally vacuous check, one
real failing control, one seeded false-positive challenge, and one seeded
false-negative challenge. The report records whether each provider detects or
misses those cases. Aggregate pass rates cannot hide any critical obligation
failure.

## Exit and promotion criteria

0.4.0 may ship when:

1. Every required quality claim binds to an exact obligation, candidate, provider
   observation, and controller-written evidence envelope.
2. Empty, skipped, unmatched, unavailable, stale, and cross-project checks can
   never appear as executed passes.
3. Deterministic positive and negative controls replay from retained identities.
4. One representative browser journey and one API or backend journey produce
   bounded, replayable, independently verified evidence.
5. The exploratory tester runs with fresh context and findings-only authority;
   candidate mutation and self-approval attempts fail closed.
6. Status distinguishes executed, vacuous, untested, unavailable, and degraded
   evidence, and distinguishes merged, deployed, and verified-live observations
   without acquiring those authorities.
7. False-positive, false-negative, flake, retry, operator-time, latency, cost,
   and artifact-size measurements are recorded for the field trials.
8. A core-only installation imports and runs its offline suite without any
   quality-provider dependency.
9. Compatibility, migration, rollback, packaging, accessibility where relevant,
   and public-boundary gates pass.

The 0.5.0 Review Canvas may depend on these artifacts only after their identities
and evidence-state semantics are stable enough to display without inventing a
second quality or approval language.

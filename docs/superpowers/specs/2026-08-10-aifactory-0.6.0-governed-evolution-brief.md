# AIFactory 0.6.0 governed evolution brief

**Date:** 2026-08-10

**Status:** Roadmap brief; later detailed design approval required

**Depends on:** 0.3.0 design authority and 0.5.0 managed ownership boundaries

## Objective

Use implementation drift and historical outcomes to propose improvements while
preventing AIFactory from changing its own active policy, prompts, analyzers, or
configuration without explicit evaluation and human admission.

## User outcomes

- An operator can see where implementation diverges from approved design.
- Repeated failures or overrides become reviewable hypotheses rather than hidden
  behavior changes.
- Proposed improvements are evaluated against a versioned replay corpus.
- Every admitted change has provenance, measured effects, rollout scope, and a
  rollback path.

## Planned scope

### Code-to-design sensor

A read-only sensor extracts a candidate structural description from the current
repository and compares it with the approved Design IR. It reports added,
removed, or changed components, interfaces, flows, dependencies, and security
boundaries as findings.

The extraction is an observation. It cannot replace Design IR, rewrite the
Contract, approve drift, or declare the implementation correct. Unsupported or
ambiguous code reports uncertainty rather than guessing.

### Improvement proposals

Eligible inputs include repeated typed failures, artifact-bound finding
overrides, review outcomes, capability shortfalls, migration incidents, and
authenticated managed-state drift. A proposal states:

- the observed pattern and bounded evidence set;
- the proposed policy, prompt, analyzer, or workflow change;
- expected benefit and safety regressions;
- affected versions and rollout scope;
- evaluation and rollback requirements.

Raw prompts, secrets, private tool transcripts, unrelated repository content,
and unauthenticated telemetry are ineligible.

### Replay evaluation

A versioned corpus contains sanitized, consented, artifact-bound cases with
expected invariants and dispositions. Candidate changes run offline against a
frozen corpus. Reports include exact candidate and corpus digests, per-case
changes, regressions, uncertainty, and aggregate measures. Aggregate improvement
cannot hide a critical invariant regression.

### Admission lifecycle

The lifecycle is propose, review, evaluate, approve, stage, observe, promote, or
roll back. Every transition is an explicit event. Active behavior references an
immutable admitted version. A proposal cannot edit active files or select its
own promotion.

## Authority and security

- Historical frequency is evidence, not authority.
- A human override is not automatically proof that a safety rule is wrong.
- Training/evaluation separation and corpus provenance are explicit.
- Runtime models cannot add cases, change expected outcomes, or promote a
  candidate during its own evaluation.
- Sanitization and public/private boundaries apply before evidence becomes
  reusable.
- Admission requires exact candidate, corpus, and evaluation digests.

## Non-goals

- Online self-training;
- automatic prompt or policy mutation;
- autonomous promotion;
- collecting unrestricted user telemetry;
- treating re-extracted design as authority;
- cross-installation aggregate learning.

## Entry criteria

- Design IR and decision replay are stable across real projects.
- Managed ownership reliably distinguishes product drift from user changes.
- A documented data-minimization and consent policy defines eligible evidence.
- Baseline evaluation cases cover the existing authority and compatibility
  invariants.

## Adversarial evidence

Tests must cover poisoned cases, prompt injection, forged provenance,
cherry-picked corpora, duplicate leakage, stale expectations, metric gaming,
critical-regression masking, false design drift, rollback failure, and attempts
by a candidate to mutate its evaluator or active version.

## Exit and promotion criteria

0.6.0 may ship when:

1. Code-to-design reports are bounded, uncertainty-aware, and non-authoritative.
2. Replay is reproducible from exact candidate and corpus digests.
3. Critical invariants cannot be traded for aggregate score gains.
4. No proposal can mutate or promote itself.
5. Staged activation and rollback are exercised end to end.
6. Evidence eligibility, sanitization, consent, retention, and deletion behavior
   are documented and tested.
7. Compatibility, packaging, and public-boundary gates pass.

Portable knowledge in 0.7.0 may use only reviewed, provenance-bearing records;
it may not turn the evolution corpus into an implicit memory store.
